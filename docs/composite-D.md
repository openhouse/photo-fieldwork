# Composite D

Composite D keeps Revision D as the receiving architecture and integrates the strongest compatible contracts surfaced across `feature/revision-A` through `feature/revision-N`. This is a provenance map of ideas, not a claim that every branch was merged wholesale.

## Composition rule

Prefer a small number of interoperable trust artifacts over parallel release systems. Keep selection, evaluation, decision history, release identity, catalog mutation, and publication authority distinct. Require every production receipt to identify the same unchanged candidate.

## Resulting trust chain

1. Deterministic selection creates a proposed master and HOLD.
2. Frozen, view-bound feedback is visually reviewed and evaluated.
3. Material decisions enter a hash-chained ledger bound to the catalog plan run; asset-specific human safety clearances are reconciled to a frozen pre-clearance baseline.
4. A final holdout is checked for asset and cluster contamination.
5. Release audit recomputes evaluation, validation, and split contamination from the current tuning, final-holdout, and canary manifests and verifies the exact catalog plan.
6. A deterministic release seal binds the config, feedback, master membership, exact assignments, HOLD, source, ledger, holdout, and reports.
7. The Apple Photos bridge refuses drift and emits test and production plans carrying that release identity.
8. The permissioned writer copies the identity into its receipt.
9. A separate read-only verifier checks exact catalog membership and emits a structured receipt carrying the same identity.

## Authority boundary

The chain can establish that a particular editor-field candidate passed declared checks and was written as planned. It cannot establish authorship, consent, rights, factual project provenance, public suitability, or publication approval. `publication_clearance` remains false throughout this workflow, and only an identified human may record a publication approval event in a separate governed process.

## Hill-climb result

The first composite added 608 recursive variants to Revision D's 187-variant frontier. Its initial implementation passed 794 of 795. The remaining compound mutation changed assignments and configuration together in a way that preserved counts, exposing that membership identity alone was insufficient. The repair bound canonical UUID, assigned view, and safety state, then required generated view albums to match those assignments exactly.

A second expansion added holdout-report forgery combinations, bringing the exhaustive frontier to 1,307 variants. The first run passed 1,306: the new coherence checker correctly understood a genuine FAIL report, but release logic failed to block that coherent non-independent result. The repair now requires coherent counts, digests, leakage, problems, status, and independence; requires actual independence; and binds the tuning digest to the released feedback membership. The complete declared frontier passes 1,307 of 1,307 variants.

A final cluster-alias expansion first revealed a noncomposing eval fixture; that oracle was corrected before product work. The corrected baseline passed 1,370 of 1,371 and exposed that `duplicate_group` and `duplicate_group_id` could name the same relationship without comparing equal. Normalizing those aliases closes the declared frontier at 1,371 of 1,371.

Independent review then expanded the recursive frontier to 4,443 variants. The red run passed 4,438: a holdout report bound tuning membership but not changed cluster structure, and a valid human publication-approval event could enter an editor-field seal. Release audit now recomputes the complete split audit from the current tuning, holdout, and canary manifests, binds normalized membership and cluster-structure digests for all three, and refuses publication approval in this release class. That frontier passes 4,443 of 4,443.

The final symmetric expansion adds tuning, holdout, and canary cluster drift and exhausts every compatible combination in a 16,731-variant frontier. All 16,731 pass. Focused regressions additionally require Apple Photos snapshot-plan schema 3, causing old helpers to fail before mutation, and prohibit alternate view columns from diverging from the sealed `primary_view` assignments.

A final independent review found that an intact ledger from another run could satisfy the decision-chain gate, a machine-suspected asset could be changed to clear without an asset-specific human transition, and a matching but structurally incomplete writer receipt could be accepted. The red recursive frontier passed 33,113 of 33,115, while the focused receipt regression failed. The repair binds event `run_id` to catalog `plan_id`, binds a frozen pre-clearance safety baseline, reconciles every changed selected asset to an exact human `safety-cleared` transition, and validates completion plus object identifiers in writer receipts. Adding the missing-clearance mutation closes the declared frontier at 65,883 of 65,883.

This result is bounded: it covers the synthetic mutation bank in this repository, not every possible archive, operator error, platform failure, or rights question.
